AI exposure and jobs

Which occupations are exposed to AI, and how has employment in them developed since ChatGPT was released in November 2022? The first tab shows my occupation-level measures; the next tabs combine them with official employment data for Norway, Sweden, the EU and the United States, and update themselves when new data are published. Occupations are grouped as in my job market paper: high or low AI exposure (above or below the median occupation's 2024 DAIOE, 35.0) and high or low task complementarity (above or below the median, 0.323), giving four exposure–complementarity quadrants. Everything here is descriptive: the comparison shows how exposed and less exposed occupations have moved, not what AI caused.

Each point is one of 422 four-digit ISCO-08 occupations. The horizontal axis is the Dynamic AI Occupational Exposure index (DAIOE, all applications); the vertical axis is the task-complementarity index, which measures how much the occupation's tasks sit in contexts where human input remains valuable. Colours and the dashed lines are the paper's quadrants: the medians of 2024 DAIOE (35.0) and of complementarity (0.323). With C-DAIOE on the horizontal axis only the complementarity line is drawn. The year menu moves the points to the provisional 2025 and 2026 values (see "Updated exposure measures" on the Norway tab) without reclassifying them. The complementarity-adjusted index C-DAIOE used in my job market paper discounts exposure by complementarity, so the occupations where AI is most likely to substitute for labor are those in the lower-right quadrant.

About the exposure data

DAIOE is the Dynamic AI Occupational Exposure index of Engberg, Görg, Lodefalk et al., which combines AI benchmark progress across applications with the abilities required in each occupation. The published series ends in 2023; the 2024 values extend it using benchmark progress from the Papers with Code archive, as described in the paper's appendix, and 2025 and 2026 extend it further with the same rule and Epoch AI's benchmarking data (provisional, not in the paper). The complementarity index follows the task-context approach of Pizzinelli et al. (2023). Scores are shown for the 422 occupations with Norwegian employment in the paper's sample; armed forces occupations are excluded. The underlying file is data/exposure_scores.json (the paper's 2024 values alone are in data/exposure_2024.json).

Updated exposure measures

The paper's DAIOE series runs to 2024. Here it is extended to 2025 and, provisionally, to 2026, with the same rule as the 2024 step: each AI application's subindex grows with the best benchmark result to date, using the Papers with Code archive (results up to July 2025) and Epoch AI's benchmarking data (dated by model release). The chart shows average exposure of Norwegian employment (2022 weights). 2025 and 2026 are provisional, and 2026 uses results released so far this year.

A factor of 1.00 with no benchmarks means no new results were available, so the subindex is held at the previous year's level. No current benchmark covers translation, Go, or (in 2026) images and speech, so the 2026 value understates progress in those applications.

Are jobs changing in ways that matter for AI?

O*NET re-rates the abilities, tasks and work context of a share of occupations in every release. Holding the AI frontier fixed at 2025, the chart recomputes exposure with each O*NET release from 19.0 (2014) to 31.0 (2026). A rising line means that occupations, as O*NET describes them, have moved towards the abilities and tasks where AI has advanced; a falling line means the opposite. Ability-only DAIOE uses the ability profile alone; C-DAIOE also uses the tasks and their complementarity. Both are indexed to the paper's release (24.3 = 100) and averaged over Norwegian employment.

Which abilities and skills is employment moving towards?

A first look at the skill set of Norwegian employment. Each occupation keeps its O*NET profile (release 24.3, the paper's), and each of O*NET's 52 abilities and 35 skills gets the employment-weighted average importance across occupations every quarter. When employment shifts towards occupations where an ability matters more, its weighted importance rises. This captures shifts between occupations only; changes in what each job requires are in the O*NET-release section above. The horizontal axis is how much the ability is exposed to AI at the 2025 frontier (the weight it carries in DAIOE, scaled 0–100).

How it is built

What this is not. The groups differ in many ways besides AI exposure. The most exposed occupations are office, finance and IT jobs, which are also sensitive to interest rates, and the post-ChatGPT period coincides with the rise in interest rates from 2022 and the end of the pandemic recovery. A gap between groups is a pattern to explain, not an effect of AI.

Employment and earnings. SSB table 11658: employees (lønnstakere), all sexes, by four-digit STYRK-08 occupation and age, and median monthly earnings, quarterly from 2016, from the a-ordningen register. STYRK-08 equals ISCO-08 at four digits. The page uses the balanced panel of occupations with counts in every quarter and age group, which covers the share of employees shown above; SSB rounds counts of one and two. Self-employed workers are not in the table. Groups follow the paper: high or low exposure relative to the unweighted median of 2024 DAIOE across occupations (35.04), high or low complementarity relative to the unweighted median of the task-complementarity index (0.323). They are fixed, so they do not move when new quarters arrive.

Other countries. Sweden: SCB occupational register, employees 16–64 by four-digit SSYK 2012 and age, annual (tables YREG51N for 2019–2021 and YREG51BAS from 2020, chained at 2020; employees with an unidentified occupation are spread across occupations in proportion to the coded ones, because occupation coding improved over these years). SSYK codes get the mean score of the ISCO-08 codes they map to in SCB's key. EU: Eurostat Labour Force Survey (lfsa_egai2d), employed persons 15–64 by two-digit ISCO-08, annual, EU-27 and each country; the LFS was redesigned in 2021. US: Census Bureau Current Population Survey, basic monthly public-use files, wage and salary employees 16 and over by Census occupation code, with composite weights, as twelve-month averages; the October 2025 survey was not carried out. Census codes are scored directly from the SOC-based scores (no ISCO crosswalk), and 2010 codes before 2020 are mapped to 2018 codes. Groups use the paper's medians everywhere.

Finer ages and skill set. SSB table 14160 (employees by two-digit occupation and seven age groups, quarterly from 2021). The skill-set measure uses O*NET 24.3 importance ratings mapped to ISCO-08 through the paper's SOC crosswalk and SSB employment from table 11658; growth rates are between four-quarter averages, as above.

Exposure. DAIOE (Engberg et al.) links AI benchmark progress in nine applications to the abilities each occupation requires. C-DAIOE, from my paper, reallocates each occupation's ability exposure across its O*NET tasks and discounts it where the task context makes AI more likely to complement than replace the worker. 2010–2024 are the values used in the paper. The 2025 and 2026 extension and the O*NET-release comparison are new on this page and are not in the paper; the scripts are extend_daioe_frontier.py, prepare_onet_vintages.py and run_cdaioe_vintage.py. In the O*NET-release comparison the AI progress series, the composite weights, the yearly scale and the three cross-occupation normalising constants are all held at the paper's values, so only the occupation descriptions change.

Updates. A scheduled job in this site's repository queries SSB, SCB, Eurostat and the Census Bureau every week and rebuilds data/ai_jobs.json and data/countries.json when new data appear. The exposure scores (data/exposure_scores.json) are rebuilt by hand when new benchmark results or a new O*NET release come out, about once a year. Sources: Statistics Norway; Statistics Sweden; Eurostat; U.S. Census Bureau; O*NET 19.0–31.0 (U.S. Department of Labor, CC BY 4.0); Epoch AI, Capabilities and benchmarking (CC BY 4.0); Papers with Code archive (CC BY-SA 4.0).
